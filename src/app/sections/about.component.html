I'm a backend engineer. I work on the unglamorous middle of software — the services, APIs, queues and schemas that a product actually runs on.

Most of my week goes into the core of a mortgage lending and brokerage platform: building REST APIs across its loan origination and back-office services in Node.js and NestJS, integrating flows like documents, e-signature and third-party data orders, working through bugs that come in from Jira, and making slow endpoints faster. It's a big multi-tenant codebase, so a lot of the job is extending something that already exists without breaking the parts around it.

The other slice is applied AI on the same platform — guideline PDFs turned into structured underwriting rules on AWS Bedrock, and a RAG assistant that answers guideline questions with a citation. It's team-built; I've worked on the services around it and on making its answers better. What I've taken from it is that the model is the easy part — the engineering is in making the output predictable enough to trust.

Either way I'm rarely working alone. Most of what I ship touches front-end, QA, DevOps or design before it's done, and the part that actually decides whether it lands on time is how well that conversation goes — asking early, flagging blockers before they're urgent, and being easy to hand work to.

Before this I spent two years on banking systems — a loan disbursement platform in Java and Spring Boot, severity-1 defects against a clock, and security findings to close before release. That work taught me to care about the boring parts: honest validation, and knowing exactly what changed between two runs.
